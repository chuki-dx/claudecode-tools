What to do when you hit your Claude Code usage limit: where it shows, when it resets, and whether to wait or add more

Published: October 17, 2026 ・ Chuki DX Lab ・ Content is based on the official docs and other sources as of writing (October 2026); sources are listed at the end of each section

Key takeaways

  • When you hit the limit, Claude Code (Anthropic's AI coding agent that runs in the terminal) stops further requests until the reset time shown. First, check your status with /usage.
  • To keep working, you can wait, switch models (if you hit an Opus or Sonnet limit), enable usage credits, or upgrade your plan. Usage credits are billed at standard API rates, separately from your subscription.
  • Team and Enterprise plans are handled differently from individual plans. Before you distribute it company-wide, check your own plan and whether usage credits are enabled.

The Pro and Max limits are a five-hour limit and a weekly limit, and they are shared with Claude.

According to the official Help Center, Pro and Max have a five-hour session limit and a weekly limit, and Max has a separate weekly limit for Fable. These limits are shared between Claude and Claude Code, so usage in both counts toward the same limits. A warning appears as you approach a limit. The following is based on what was documented as of writing (October 2026).

If you hit the session limit in the middle of a response, work may continue briefly to finish the step in progress, and Usage limit reached · wrapping up is shown. This grace is small and capped, and what you use counts toward the weekly limit. It applies only to a response already in progress, not to new messages you send after hitting the limit. On Pro it may apply at most once per weekly usage period; on Max it may apply each time you hit the session limit, within the weekly limit. It is being rolled out gradually, so some accounts don't have it yet.

Source: Use Claude Code with your Pro or Max plan

How to read the limit message and reset time

According to the official docs, when you use up your subscription usage allowance, you see one of the following messages.

You've hit your session limit · resets 3:45pm
You've hit your weekly limit · resets Mon 12:00am
You've hit your Opus limit · resets 3:45pm
You've hit your Sonnet limit · resets 3:45pm

Claude Code stops sending additional requests until the reset time shown in the message. The session and weekly limits apply to all models, so switching models doesn't let you keep working. The Opus and Sonnet limits apply only to their own model families, so you can continue by switching to a model outside that family with /model. However, each model has its own cache, so the next request rereads the entire conversation and the cache doesn't help.

During intensive work such as running large workflows in parallel, you may use up your weekly limit before the session limit resets.

Confirm and wait actions

  • Use /usage to check your plan limits and reset time.
  • With /usage-credits, Pro and Max users can buy extra usage, and Team and Enterprise users can request it from an admin.
  • As you approach the limit, you may see a warning such as You've used 85% of your session limit · resets 3:45pm.
  • In interactive sessions signed in with a claude.ai subscription, it can also wait in the open session and resume the interrupted task after the limit resets. Before v2.1.234, this waiting did not exist.
  • In the Code tab of the Desktop app, the session limit card has an Auto-continue when limits reset checkbox (the weekly limit card doesn't). It's a separate setting from Continue automatically at usage limit in the CLI's /config, so to turn it off, do it in each place.

Source: Error reference

Wait or add more: the options the official docs list for Pro and Max

These are the options the official Help Center lists for when you hit a limit. Upgrading is described as an option if you "always hit the limit"; if you only hit it occasionally, waiting is also an option.

On the Pro plan

  • If you constantly hit the limit and need more capacity for large repositories, consider Max 5x
  • Turn on usage credits and keep using Pro after reaching the five-hour session limit or the weekly limit
  • Switch to a Claude Console account and buy API usage credits for intensive coding
  • Wait for the limit to reset

On the Max plan

  • If you always hit the limit on Max 5x, consider Max 20x
  • Enable usage credits to keep working after you reach the session limit, the regular weekly limit, and the separate weekly limit for Fable
  • Buy API usage credits in the Claude Console, or wait for the limit to reset

Note that the monthly API credits included with Max, including extra usage, cannot be used in Claude Code or the Claude app.

Source: Use Claude Code with your Pro or Max plan

How usage credits work, how to configure them, and cost considerations

According to the official Help Center, usage credits let individual Pro, Max 5x, and Max 20x subscribers keep working after reaching their included limit by switching to pay-as-you-go billing at standard API rates. If enabled, you are not stopped at the limit, and requests are processed with usage credits. You configure it in your Claude account under Settings > Usage.

  1. Click "Enable" in the Usage credits section, and register a payment method if you haven't set one up
  2. Set the monthly cap with "Adjust limit" (you can also choose no limit with "Set to unlimited")
  3. Prepay with "Add funds." You can also set up auto-reload, which buys more automatically when your balance falls below a threshold

Notes

  • Usage credits are billed separately from the subscription and appear on the invoice as an additional charge. Check the official pricing page for the latest amounts.
  • Usage continues until you reach the monthly limit, or until the balance runs out if auto-reload is off.
  • At the moment of switching, the "approaching your limit" notice may linger, and it changes to Now using credits once the next request goes through. Nothing has stopped.
  • The limit reset timing does not change. If you subscribed through the mobile app, enabling and purchasing are available only on the web version.
  • In most cases they do not expire, but in some regions, including Japan, they expire six months after purchase, starting 2026-09-10. You will receive an email 7 days before expiration.

Source: Manage usage credits for paid Claude plans

How to think about distributing it on Team and Enterprise

According to the official Help Center, usage-based Enterprise plans (including self-serve) have no per-seat usage limits and are billed at API rates based on usage.

On the Team plan and seat-based Enterprise plans, enabling usage credits lets members keep working in Claude, Cowork, and Claude Code after they reach the included limit. The monthly API credits included in the Team plan can't be used in Claude Code or the Claude app. Which plan you're on and whether to enable usage credits affect billing, so check before you roll this out.

Source: Use Claude Code with your Team or Enterprise plan

FAQ

When do Claude Code limits reset?

When you hit a limit, the message shows the reset time. For the weekly limit, for example, it looks like You've hit your weekly limit · resets Mon 12:00am, and requests stop until that time. You can also check your plan's limits and reset times with /usage.

Source: Error reference

If I see Server is temporarily limiting requests, have I hit my limit?

No. According to the official docs, it is a short-lived server-side limit unrelated to your plan limits. From v2.1.199 onward, it is automatically retried with backoff before being shown. Wait a moment and try again, and if it continues, check the official status page.

Source: Error reference

Can the monthly API credit included with Max be used for usage beyond the limit?

No. According to the official Help Center, the monthly API credit included with Max, including extra usage, can't be used in Claude Code or the Claude app. To keep working after you hit the limit, consider other options such as usage credits.

Source: Use Claude Code with your Pro or Max plan
